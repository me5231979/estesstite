Leadership Education

CertificatesResults

Why it matters now

Most senior leadership programs end at graduation. People get a certificate, go back to the same problems, and nothing in the business moves. Leaders now face change faster than any curriculum can be rewritten, so a program has to produce decisions and results, not attendance.

How I transform it
  1. 01

    Executive-led

    Executives sponsor it, teach in it, and judge the outcomes. Leaders learn from the people who run the business, not from slides about it.

  2. 02

    Results-based

    Every leader brings a real business problem. The capstone is the curriculum; modules and coaching exist to move that problem forward.

  3. 03

    Impact-driven

    Capstones are pitched to the executive team, then applied back at work with a named owner and a check months later on what actually changed.

What it looks like

A cohort program built around one capstone per leader. Modules, coaching, and weekly synthesis all feed it. It ends with a pitch to executives, and the work keeps running in operations after the program is over.

What changes
  • Leaders leave with a result, not a certificate.
  • Executives see the return in work that shipped.
  • Capstone projects keep running after the program ends.

Learning at the speed of change

Six-month buildsHours

Why it matters now

A custom course used to take months. By the time it launched, the need had moved. When skills change this fast, the old production model guarantees learning that arrives late.

How I transform it
  1. 01

    Run it like product

    Treat course development like product development: a tight intake, a working version fast, then iterate.

  2. 02

    Build with AI

    Use AI coding tools to build complete interactive learning sites, not slide decks with a quiz at the end.

  3. 03

    Standards built in

    Brand and adult-learning standards live inside the process, so speed never depends on cutting corners or on who is at the keyboard.

What it looks like

An intake on the one task that's going wrong, then a 10 to 45 minute interactive course built in a few hours of working time, ready while the need is still live.

What changes
  • Learning lands while the need is still live.
  • Quality holds no matter who builds it.
  • Subject experts can build with guardrails instead of waiting in a queue.

03 / 07

Manager Effectiveness

One-time trainingA measured standard

Why it matters now

Managers decide most of what people experience at work: engagement, growth, whether they stay. Yet most manager training is a one-time event with no definition of a good manager and no way to tell if anyone improved.

How I transform it
  1. 01

    Define it

    Write down what good management looks like, anchored to research on leadership behavior, in language managers recognize.

  2. 02

    Measure it

    A short employee pulse on those behaviors, so improvement is visible team by team.

  3. 03

    Close the gap

    Learning starts the day someone becomes a manager, and is tested against a comparison group to prove it worked.

What it looks like

A written manager standard, a five-question employee pulse, learning assigned automatically on promotion, and a cohort trial with a built-in comparison group.

What changes
  • Everyone knows what good looks like.
  • You can see whether managers actually got better.
  • Manager development stops being an event and becomes a system.

04 / 07

Internal Mobility

Hidden rolesVisible growth

Why it matters now

People leave when they can't see where they could grow. The best ones grow somewhere else. Mobility is retention with a strategy.

How I transform it
  1. 01

    Make skills visible

    Skills profiles and growth plans that speak the same language as the job architecture.

  2. 02

    Make opportunities findable

    An opportunity marketplace where open roles, mentors, and projects are easy to see and easy to raise a hand for.

  3. 03

    Let people try first

    Short-term gigs, so people can test a new role before they apply for it.

What it looks like

A talent platform with skills profiles, growth plans, and an internal marketplace tied to a skills-based job architecture, launched with a staff strategy and communications that got people to use it.

What changes
  • Talent moves inside instead of walking out.
  • Skills data shows which skills are rising and falling.
  • Growth becomes something people can see and act on.

05 / 07

Talent Review and Succession

Gut feelOne method

Why it matters now

Talent reviews often run on instinct and live in one leader's head. When that leader leaves, the succession plan leaves with them, and decisions differ from one part of the organization to the next.

How I transform it
  1. 01

    One method

    One calibration approach, run the same way everywhere, so decisions are comparable.

  2. 02

    Tied to skills

    Linked to critical roles and the skills architecture, not just to performance ratings.

  3. 03

    Private by design

    Built so managers see their own people, not their peers, which keeps the conversation honest.

What it looks like

A nine-box calibration tool tied to critical roles and skills, built identically for every part of the organization, feeding succession plans.

What changes
  • Consistent talent decisions across the organization.
  • Bench strength you can actually see.
  • Succession that survives a leader leaving.

06 / 07

AI Fluency

"Learn AI"Know where you stand

Why it matters now

Everyone has been told to learn AI. Almost nobody knows where they stand or what to take first. And AI changes the work itself, so training people on tools without redesigning the work is theater.

How I transform it
  1. 01

    Diagnose first

    A self-assessment built on a validated research scale that scores each person instantly.

  2. 02

    Recommend second

    Every person gets a personal learning path from their score, with a dedicated track for managers.

  3. 03

    Redesign the work

    Map AI exposure role by role, so learning follows the work that is actually changing.

What it looks like

A fully automated AI literacy assessment: take it, get your score, and receive your own learning path the same minute, plus a manager version built on the same model.

What changes
  • People start where they actually are.
  • Learning follows the jobs AI is changing.
  • Leaders get a real picture of readiness, not a completion count.

07 / 07

Practice-Based Learning

Learning by mistakeRehearsal

Why it matters now

Managers usually learn the hardest conversations by getting them wrong with a real person. The cost lands on employees, on trust, and sometimes on legal risk.

How I transform it
  1. 01

    Find the hard moment

    Start from the moments in the workflow where mistakes are expensive.

  2. 02

    Simulate it

    Build a simulation with real stakes, including the details that differ by situation and jurisdiction.

  3. 03

    Make it push back

    Add AI role-play so the other person responds the way a real one would.

What it looks like

A progressive-discipline simulator that walks a manager through the conversation, with legal differences built in, so the first time they have it is not the first time they've practised it.

What changes
  • People rehearse the hard moment before it counts.
  • Fewer costly mistakes with real employees.
  • Confidence that shows up in the real conversation.
