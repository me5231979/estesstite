The role, honestly

A Vanderbilt manager holds three jobs at once.

Most managers were promoted because they were excellent at the work. The role that follows is a different craft. Pick a domain to begin — each ends in a simulator where you make the calls a real manager makes. And beneath all three runs a fourth thing: the human craft that makes the other jobs work.

Domain 01 · Compliance & Duty · ~15 min

The obligations that aren't optional.

01

Compliance is the floor, not the ceiling. As a manager you carry duties an individual contributor never had: you are often a mandatory reporter, a steward of safety, and the first line of a legal obligation the university owes its people. Here is the baseline.

Domain 02 · Employee Relations · ~15 min

The work is people. All of it.

02

Employee Relations (ER) is everything that happens between the policy and the person: the coaching, the conflict, the conversation you'd rather not have. Managers who do this well are made, not born — and the making is mostly reps.

Simulator · Handle or escalate?

Knowing your lane is a skill.

Great managers solve most things at their desk. The best ones also know the precise moment a situation stops being theirs to solve alone — and loop in Engagement Consultants (ECs), Employee Relations, or Title IX before it grows. Make the call on each.

Domain 03 · Operational Craft · ~15 min

The machinery of the role — run correctly.

03

This is where policy meets paperwork: hiring, leave, pay, PTO, expenses, and discipline. Small process errors here create large legal and financial exposure. The two most error-prone processes — FMLA and progressive discipline — each get a full simulator.

Baseline operational responsibilities

Flagship simulator · FMLA

The FMLA Simulator

An employee comes to you with a life event. What you say — and don't say — in the next five minutes can protect the university or expose it to an interference claim. FMLA rarely arrives labeled "FMLA." Walk the full flow: recognize, respond, refer, and protect.

Flagship simulator · Progressive discipline

The Progressive Discipline Simulator

A capable employee's performance is slipping. You want to fix it, not fire someone — but if it comes to that, the record has to hold up. Walk the ladder: coach, document, warn, and know exactly when to bring in Employee Relations. Consistency is the whole game.

The Mastery Track · Six advanced modules

Foundations first. Mastery now.

The foundations covered the moments of highest legal risk. These six modules cover the rest of the craft — hiring, growing, supporting, and, when it comes to it, saying goodbye. Same rules: you make the calls, the feedback is immediate, and nothing is reported.

Mastery 01 · The Interview Room

Eight interview questions. Which would you ask?

You're hiring a program coordinator. A colleague drafted questions for the panel. Some are excellent, some carry legal risk, and a few should never be said out loud in an interview. Sort them — Ask it, Rephrase it, or Never ask.

Mastery 02 · The Calibration Lab

Spot the bias before it reaches the rating.

Review season. Six rating rationales are on the table — real quotes from real calibration meetings, lightly disguised. Diagnose each one: is it fair and specific, or is a bias doing the rating?

Mastery 03 · Simulator · Reasonable accommodation

The Accommodation Simulator

An employee asks for a change because of a medical condition. The ADA calls what happens next “the interactive process” — a structured, good-faith conversation most managers have never been taught. Walk it end to end without stepping on a legal rake.

Mastery 04 · Hybrid & remote teams

Half the team is on a screen. What breaks first?

Hybrid work quietly created two new manager traps: proximity bias (the people you see get the opportunities) and cross-state wage-and-hour exposure (remote time you never see). Sort six everyday situations.

Mastery 05 · Simulator · Leading through change

The Reorg Simulator

Leadership announces a restructure you didn’t choose and privately have doubts about. Your team finds out Thursday. How you carry the next two weeks will determine whether you keep their trust — and your credibility with leadership.

Mastery 06 · Simulator · The last conversation

The Termination Simulator

The PIP ran its course. PCB has signed off. Today is Dana’s last day — she just doesn’t know it yet. This is the hardest fifteen minutes in management. Do it with process and dignity, because your team is watching how you treat people on the way out.
