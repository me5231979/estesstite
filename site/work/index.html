01 / Learning Design · AI

AI-Experiential Learning (AXL)

~80

Courses in 12 months

Screen recordingTo capture
The problem

Custom learning took months. By launch, the need had moved.

The move

Treat course development like product development. Use AI coding tools to build complete interactive learning sites, fast.

What I built

A production model with brand standards and adult learning design built into the process. A 10 to 45 minute interactive course takes 2 to 3 hours of working time, including SME intake.

What changed

~80 courses in 12 months. No data collected from learners. Quality no longer depends on who's at the keyboard.

What I'd do next

Open the model to subject matter experts so they can build with guardrails, not wait in a queue.

02 / Leadership Development

Leadership Redefined

116

Leaders completed

Screen recordingTo capture
The problem

Senior leadership programs produce certificates. Organizations need decisions and results.

The move

Make the capstone the point. Every cohort has to ship a real project.

What I built

A Chancellor-sponsored program: 6 modules, 3 coaching sessions, weekly synthesis, and a capstone pitched to leadership. Then a half-day symposium format to reach hundreds of managers.

What changed

116 leaders completed across 3 cohorts. 94% satisfied. 98 still at the university. Capstones running in operations today.

What I'd do next

Tie every capstone to a named business owner and a 6-month outcome check.

03 / Leadership Development · Measurement

The Manager Standard

677

Managers, 1 standard

Screen recordingTo capture
The problem

Manager training was a one-time event with no definition of a good manager and no way to tell if anyone improved.

The move

Define the behavior, measure it, then build learning to close the gap.

What I built

A written standard anchored to Yukl's leadership taxonomy. A 5-item employee pulse. Learning auto-assigned on role change. A cohort trial with a built-in comparison group.

What changed

1 standard covering 677 managers across 36 business units. A system designed to answer the question most programs skip: did managers get better?

What I'd do next

Connect pulse results to retention data by team.

04 / Talent Systems · Internal Mobility

Talent Marketplace

1,031

Internal moves in 12 months

Screen recordingTo capture
The problem

Staff couldn't see where they could grow, so the best ones grew somewhere else.

The move

Make skills visible and opportunities findable.

What I built

The staff strategy and communications for a talent platform on Oracle Grow: skills profiles, growth plans, and an opportunity marketplace tied to a skills-based job architecture.

What changed

90% of staff engaged. Confirm time window Vanderbilt staff made 1,031 internal moves in 12 months.

What I'd do next

Short-term gigs, so people can test a new role before they apply for it.

05 / Talent Systems · Succession

Talent Review at Scale

36

Business units

Screen recordingTo capture
The problem

Talent reviews ran on gut feel and lived in 1 leader's head.

The move

1 standard method, tied to skills, private by design.

What I built

A 9-box calibration tool linked to critical roles and the skills architecture. 1 workbook per business unit, all built the same way, with managers unable to see their peers.

What changed

36 business units on 1 consistent process.

What I'd do next

Move it from a spreadsheet to a secure tool that feeds succession plans automatically.

06 / AI · Future of Work

AI Capability Building

Screen recordingTo capture
The problem

Everyone was told to "learn AI." Nobody knew where they stood or what to take first.

The move

Diagnose first. Recommend second.

What I built

An AI literacy self-assessment built on a validated research scale. It scores instantly and sends each person a personal learning path. Plus a manager behavior assessment on the same model.

What changed

Both run fully automated, end to end. A broader AI fluency initiative, with a dedicated manager track, is planned to launch.

What I'd do next

Map AI exposure by role so the learning follows the work that's actually changing.

07 / Simulation · Manager Readiness

Practice Before It Counts

Screen recordingTo capture
The problem

Managers learn employee relations by getting it wrong with a real person.

The move

Let them get it wrong in a simulation instead.

What I built

An employee relations and progressive discipline simulator with state-specific legal differences. An HR operations simulation is in development.

What changed

Managers rehearse the hard conversation before they have it.

What I'd do next

Add AI role-play so the employee pushes back.
