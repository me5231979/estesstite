Point of view

7 beliefs I work by.

01 / 07

01

Nobody needs another course.

They need to do the job better next month.

How I address it

Every course I build targets one thing people need to do differently at work, and gets built in hours, not months. Draft, Matt to confirm

02

Start with the work, not the learner.

Capability gaps live in the workflow, not the catalog.

How I address it

Practice tools should come straight from the workflow. If managers learn hard conversations by getting them wrong with real people, they need a simulator, not a slide deck. Draft, Matt to confirm

See the approach
03

Speed is a quality standard.

If it takes 6 months to build, the need already moved.

How I address it

Brand and adult-learning standards live inside the production process, so going faster never means cutting corners. Draft, Matt to confirm

See the model
04

Build it. Don't deck it.

A working prototype ends more debates than any slide.

How I address it

I build the first version myself: simulators, talent tools, intake pipelines. Then the team owns it. Draft, Matt to confirm

05

Measure behavior, not attendance.

Completions are vanity. Changed habits are value.

How I address it

Define the behavior, pulse employees on it, and test the learning against a comparison group. That's how you know managers actually got better. Draft, Matt to confirm

See the approach
06

Talent moves inside or it walks out.

Mobility is retention with a strategy.

How I address it

Make skills visible and opportunities findable: skills profiles, growth plans, and open roles people can actually see. Draft, Matt to confirm

07

AI is a job redesign problem.

Training people on tools without redesigning the work is theater.

How I address it

Start by showing each person where they stand, with a self-assessment that sends a personal learning path. Then map AI exposure role by role. Draft, Matt to confirm

Capability architecture

It runs on data, and it never stops.

Data shows what the work needs. The job architecture turns that into skills. Growth closes the gaps. Data shows whether it worked. Then it runs again.

Sense Map Grow Prove
  1. 1 · Sense

    What's changing?

    Skills-change data from outside. Workforce signals from inside: assessments, pulse surveys, turnover, mobility.

    What changesKnow where the work is changing before it shows up in performance.
  2. 2 · Map

    What does each role need now?

    Break each role into tasks. Rate how AI and technology change each one. Keep the job architecture current as the data moves.

    What changesEvery role tied to the skills it needs now, and next.
  3. 3 · Grow

    How do people get there?

    Never a single event. Three layers run at the same time, below.

    What changesLearning inside the work, not a catalog beside it.
  4. 4 · Prove

    Did it move?

    Behavior against a comparison group, then business results. What we learn feeds the next round of Sense.

    What changesProof in behavior and business results, not completions.

Then Sense again. The architecture is never finished.

Grow

Learning is never an event.

01

Always on

Constant engagement, so the whole workforce is always in something, between the moments that matter.

Example A recurring series everyone is in, so learning has a rhythm instead of a calendar of one-off events.

02

At the point of need

Tools inside the work, there when the task is: simulators, job aids, AI assistants, learning that starts on a role change.

Example A simulator for hard conversations, and manager learning that starts the day someone becomes a manager. See the approach

03

Somewhere to go

A talent marketplace that speaks the job architecture's skills, so growth turns into gigs, mentors, and new roles. Its data shows which skills are moving and feeds the next Sense.

Example Skills profiles, growth plans, and open roles people can actually find, with short-term gigs to try a role first. See the approach
