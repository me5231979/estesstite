Point of view

7 beliefs I work by.

01 / 07

01

Nobody needs another course.

They need to do the job better next month.

How I address it

Each AXL course targets one thing people need to do differently at work, and takes 2 to 3 hours of working time to build. Draft, Matt to confirm

See AXL
02

Start with the work, not the learner.

Capability gaps live in the workflow, not the catalog.

How I address it

The ER simulator came straight from the workflow. Managers were learning discipline conversations by getting them wrong with real people. Draft, Matt to confirm

See the simulator
03

Speed is a quality standard.

If it takes 6 months to build, the need already moved.

How I address it

Brand and adult-learning standards are built into my production model, so going faster doesn't mean cutting corners. It produced ~80 courses in 12 months. Draft, Matt to confirm

See the model
04

Build it. Don't deck it.

A working prototype ends more debates than any slide.

How I address it

I build the first version myself. The ER simulator, the talent review tool, and the intake pipeline all started on my keyboard. Draft, Matt to confirm

Enter the Lab
05

Measure behavior, not attendance.

Completions are vanity. Changed habits are value.

How I address it

The Manager Standard defines the behavior, pulses employees on it, and tests the learning against a comparison group. It covers 677 managers. Draft, Matt to confirm

See the standard
06

Talent moves inside or it walks out.

Mobility is retention with a strategy.

How I address it

I led the staff strategy and communications for a talent marketplace: skills profiles, growth plans, and open roles people can actually find. Draft, Matt to confirm

See the marketplace
07

AI is a job redesign problem.

Training people on tools without redesigning the work is theater.

How I address it

I start with an AI literacy self-assessment that scores each person and sends a learning path. Mapping AI exposure role by role comes next. Draft, Matt to confirm

See the approach

Capability architecture

It runs on data, and it never stops.

Data shows what the work needs. The job architecture turns that into skills. Growth closes the gaps. Data shows whether it worked. Then it runs again.
